Method

How an entry gets in

No evidence, no entry. This is the rule the whole ledger is built on.

What counts as an event

A dated, public, verifiable change at a company or program: a technical milestone, funding, a contract, a regulatory action, a product launch, a hiring move or a shutdown. Opinion pieces, forecasts and rumours are left out.

Sourcing

Each event carries a source link, the source title and a short excerpt copied word for word from that page. We prefer the company's own newsroom or filings, then regulators, then major outlets, then trade press. The confidence figure on each row reflects that order: 0.95 for a primary source, 0.85 for a major outlet, 0.70 for trade press. Rows below 0.50 are rejected.

How rows are collected and checked

A collector searches the web with Claude and proposes candidate rows. A validator then rejects any row without a source URL or excerpt, any duplicate (same source page, or the same company and event type with a near-identical title within ten days), and, when it can reach the page, any row whose excerpt is not on the source. Candidates go to a reviewed pull request; a person reads them before they enter the ledger. The seed rows were gathered by hand-run research and checked the same way.

The Promise Ledger

A claim is a dated public statement that something will ship by a date. We quote it and link it. Status starts at Pending. Delivered, Slipped, Dropped and On track each need a second source showing it, recorded as status evidence. We quote; we do not editorialise.

Corrections

If an entry is wrong, the source link is the fastest way to check it. Corrections replace the row and keep the source trail.

What is public and what is membership

Headline counts, the latest entries, the last 180 days of the ledger and the full Promise Ledger are open to read. Watchlist alerts, the full history and the CSV export are membership features, opening in early access.
