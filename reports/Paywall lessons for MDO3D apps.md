# Fix the gate before the price

Across all three MDO3D portfolios, the successful analogs share one rule: **give away a real, working result for the core action, then charge for depth, volume, persistence or a keepable artifact, through a payment flow buyers can trust.** MDO3D's biggest gap is not its prices. It is that the gate itself has been broken or untrustworthy. The internal review found paid flows that did not verify payment, readings lost across the Stripe redirect, no Terms, Privacy or refund pages, invented stats and testimonials, contradictory Pro pricing, and a sign-in wall before first use. Each of these is a failure the category leaders either avoid or are publicly punished for. For **divination**, the closest analogs are Co-Star's question packs and reports ($2.99–$11.99) and Astrodienst's one-time PDF reports, and they validate MDO3D's no-account, one-time model. That model also sidesteps the subscription dark patterns that define the category's worst reputations (Nebula). For **mdothree**, nobody successfully sells a subscription to a single dev formatter. The working models are Coolors-style persistence gates, Sejda-style one-time passes, sponsorship, or a funnel to something else. For **rigor.design**, $9.99/mo undercuts every major competitor ($19–$50/mo), but the free tier is too generous (21 uses/month across 7 subdomains) and the sign-in wall sits in the wrong place. Leaders show the score free and gate the fix. For a solo operator running about 28 sites, the evidence points to a short list of fixes. In order: payment integrity, truthful trust signals and legal pages, value-first gating, then pricing and traffic tests concentrated on the 2–4 sites that turn out to earn.

## Broken payment flows and fake proof are the most expensive problems

The highest-leverage lessons apply to every site, because they decide whether a willing buyer can complete a purchase and whether a paid sale stays paid. Stripe's documentation is blunt: **"You can't rely on triggering fulfillment only from your checkout landing page, because it's not guaranteed customers visit that page."** Fulfillment has to run off the `checkout.session.completed` webhook, or off a server-side retrieval of the session on the success page. Either way, the code checks `payment_status` and records fulfillment idempotently, because the function "might be called multiple times, possibly concurrently" ([Stripe: Fulfill orders](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)). The internal review's divination findings break exactly this contract: wrong verify hosts, readings lost across the redirect, and email captured through `window.prompt`. The fix pattern for a no-account app has three steps. First, persist the user's input (question, draw, birth data, resume text) server-side under a random request ID before redirecting. Second, pass that ID as `client_reference_id` on a server-created Checkout Session. Third, on the webhook, confirm the session is paid, that its price ID matches the product the request belongs to, and that the request is still unfulfilled. Stripe notes that invalid `client_reference_id` values are **"silently dropped, but your payment page continues to work"**, so a malformed ID creates orphan payments that look like successes ([Stripe: Payment Link URL parameters](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links/url-parameters)). Checkout already collects the buyer's email. Emailing a signed permalink to the result replaces the `window.prompt` hack and covers the "paid, then lost connection" case.

The fee and dispute math explains why reliability matters more than price. At $2.99, Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ takes **12.9% of revenue**, and every dispute costs **$15**, which is the gross of about five readings ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). A merchant of record such as Paddle at 5% + 50¢ would take about 22% of a $2.99 sale ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). Plain Stripe is therefore the right default for a US seller with mostly US buyers, until EU VAT filing becomes a burden. A buyer who pays and receives nothing is the textbook dispute. A generous, published refund policy ("full refund if the reading fails to generate or on request within 7 days") costs far less than the chargebacks it prevents.

Fabricated social proof is now a legal liability, not just a trust problem. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials took effect on **October 21, 2024**. It explicitly bans **AI-generated** testimonials, testimonials from people without real experience, and fake social-influence metrics, with civil penalties of roughly **$52K per violation** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). The "50K+ readings" counter, the testimonials on the guidance hub, and rigor's "94% ATS pass rate" hero stat should come down unless they are measured and documented. The analogs that earn trust use signals that can be checked: named, credentialed authors (Astrodienst's Liz Greene, described with doctorates and Jungian training) ([Astro.com](https://www.astro.com/prod/pr_reportsgreene_e.htm?lang=e)), longevity (Cafe Astrology since 1996) ([Cafe Astrology](https://cafeastrology.com/category/about/)), verifiable privacy architecture (JSONLint's "data never leaves your machine," backed by an open-source engine) ([JSONLint](https://jsonlint.com/about)), and real app-store ratings. An anonymous AI micro-site has none of the first two. Its usable trust signals are transparent pricing, a visible refund policy, an honest AI disclosure, an entertainment disclaimer, Stripe-hosted checkout, and a sample of the paid output.

Stripe's website checklist effectively defines the minimum legal page set. It asks for a clear description of what is sold, the currency, **direct contact channels "besides contact forms,"** a refund policy, a cancellation policy for subscriptions, a privacy policy, and trial and promotion terms. Stripe also offers its Shop Terms under CC BY 4.0 as a starting template ([Stripe website checklist](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/checklist/website)). It recommends telling buyers on the site what statement descriptor they will see, which cuts down on "I don't recognize this charge" disputes ([Stripe account checklist](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/checklist)). For the two subscription portfolios, the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated on **July 8, 2025** ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated-ahead-of-planned-july-14-effective-date)). The FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM on **March 11, 2026** ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/ftc-kicks-new-negative-options-rulemaking)), and California's amended auto-renewal law has applied since **July 1, 2025**. That law requires express consent with proof kept for three years, annual reminders, and online cancellation through the same medium used to sign up ([Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025)). Following the California standard nationally is cheap: renewal terms next to the pay button, a receipt with the cancel link, and a Stripe Customer Portal link emailed so it works without an account.

## Divination: one-time readings fit, but only if the paid reading visibly uses the buyer's own draw

The category leaders all give away the daily ritual and charge for specificity. Co-Star gives away daily horoscopes, the birth chart and compatibility. It sells **5 Questions for $2.99, 10 for $4.99, 25 for $6.99**, plus single reports from $6.99 to $11.99, next to an $8.99/mo subscription ([Co-Star App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/co-star-personalized-astrology/id1264782561)). Co-Star and Labyrinthos have both added **paid AI readings for open-ended questions** ([PBS NewsHour/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/more-tarot-readers-are-turning-to-ai-for-advice-another-sign-of-our-growing-reliance-on-chatbots-for-emotional-support)). That validates MDO3D's product shape at MDO3D's price points, from the category's most-rated app. Web-first incumbents use the model closest to MDO3D's: free calculators and content, with one-time PDF reports sold next to the free output ([Astro.com](https://www.astro.com/prod/pr_reportsgreene_e.htm?lang=e); [Cafe Astrology](https://cafeastrology.com/category/about/)).

Nebula is the cautionary tale and also MDO3D's best marketing contrast. Its trial auto-converts to a weekly charge. Trustpilot reviewers describe a $42 charge "without consent," repeat charges after cancelling twice, and a "My Plan" screen that spins with no way to unsubscribe ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/appnebula.co?page=9)). Another review says users were "repeatedly directed to new pages to extract more money" after checkout ([Medium](https://medium.com/@emeldawheary61/the-astrology-app-that-quietly-emptied-my-wallet-nebula-review-697ef2defb02)). A no-account, one-time Stripe payment avoids negative-option and auto-renewal exposure entirely, and "one payment, no subscription, nothing to cancel" answers the category's loudest complaint directly. In a low-quality but suggestive roundup, Stellium's one-time unlock is "consistently praised" ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/co-star-sanctuary-pattern-nebula-stellium-astrology-apps-ranked-2026)).

The main perceived-value risk is generic output. Tarot practitioners criticize AI readings because they "tend to be generic" and "interpretation is the whole point of tarot" ([PBS NewsHour/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/more-tarot-readers-are-turning-to-ai-for-advice-another-sign-of-our-growing-reliance-on-chatbots-for-emotional-support)). That makes the review's finding that **free readings ignored user input** the most damaging product bug in the portfolio. A free reading that ignores the question teaches the visitor that the paid one will too. The paid reading has to cite the actual cards and positions, the hexagram and its changing lines, the birth data, or the dream's details. It needs visible structure ("what this means," "what to do next"), and it should arrive as something the buyer keeps: an emailed permalink or PDF, the way Astrodienst delivers reports. A short preview of the premium reading, generated from the buyer's own draw, is the soft-gate pattern. RevenueCat's data shows why the gate should be soft. Hard paywalls convert about 5x better on mobile, but **hard-gated apps see refund rates 70% higher** ([Subscription Insider/RevenueCat 2025](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)). At $15 per dispute, MDO3D cannot afford that refund profile.

On price, the evidence argues against defaulting to $2.99. RevenueCat found that higher-priced apps convert downloads to trials at 8.9% versus 4.4% for low-priced ones and earn **$62.19 versus $10.69** in annual LTV per payer ([SaaStr/RevenueCat 2026](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). Combined with the 30¢ fixed fee, that points to a **$4.99–$6.99 single reading** with a 3-pack (for example $9.99) shared across sister sites. This is an inference from mobile data, not a tested web benchmark. Two compliance edges round this out. Stripe lists "psychic services and fortune tellers" as prohibited only in Japan, Mexico and Thailand, not globally ([Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). And an entertainment-and-reflection disclaimer plus an AI-generated disclosure belong on every reading page.

## mdothree: nobody pays $4.99 for a timestamp converter, but people pay for saved state and batch work

The utility analogs split into three models, and none of them charge for the core single action. The first is **hard usage caps plus a subscription**. Smallpdf allows 2 tasks/day ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/smallpdf/pricing)), and iLovePDF caps batch size and file size, then charges **$5/mo annually or $9/mo monthly** for 4 GB files, unlimited processing and no ads ([iLovePDF pricing](https://www.ilovepdf.com/pricing)). The second is **no limits, ad-funded**. PDF24 is entirely free and ad-supported ([PDF24](https://help.pdf24.org/en/?p=332)), and iLovePDF grew bootstrapped to **150M+ monthly users** without paid ads ([iLovePDF about](https://www.ilovepdf.com/de/hilfe/uber-uns)). The third is **persistence and power-feature gates on a cheap plan**. Coolors' free tier caps saved palettes at 10 and palette size at 5 colors and shows "ads and popups." Pro is advertised at **$3/month** with no trial and a renewal reminder 3 days before each charge ([Coolors Pro](https://coolors.co/pro)). That makes Coolors the single closest analog to mdothree's price point.

The dev-utility evidence is consistent and unflattering for a pure subscription. Every high-traffic dev utility checked earns through something other than a paywall on the tool itself. JSONLint runs Carbon Ads and sells a Mac app ([JSONLint](https://jsonlint.com/about)). regex101, with about **70,000 unique visitors a day**, runs on sponsors, a supporter tier and ads ([regex101](https://docs.regex101.com/enterprise/sponsors/)). crontab.guru exists to send roughly 120K monthly organic visitors toward Cronitor's paid monitoring product ([marketingexamples](https://marketingexamples.com/seo/cronitor)). Indie JSON tools launch on "no ads, no tracking, no signup" and plan to add "ethical ads" later ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/just-launched-formatjsononline-com-a-clean-fast-and-private-json-formatter-MKiSTl0H9iteAOFE4ACc)). The implication for mdothree is that **Pro must be one bundle across every subdomain** and must sell things that cost nothing to enforce client-side: history and saved items synced across tools, batch hashing and multi-file PDF/image jobs, presets, export formats, offline PWA, and no ads. The color, PDF and image tools carry the strongest case for paid, because those users have recurring workflows. The hash, timestamp and JSON tools should plan on a Carbon-style sponsor slot as the revenue floor and treat Pro as upside. A Sejda-style **one-time $2–3 week pass that does not auto-renew** suits one-off PDF and image users who distrust subscriptions ([Sejda pricing](https://www.sejda.com/pricing)).

Settling on one price is a precondition, not a detail. Showing $4 in one place and $4.99 in another, or a trial in one place and none in another, conflicts with Stripe's requirement that it be clear what is sold and on what terms ([Stripe website checklist](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/checklist/website)). It also conflicts with California's requirement of clear renewal terms. The utility category's worst failures are trial-to-annual conversions (Smallpdf reviewers describe a one-week trial auto-converting to a **$108 annual charge**) ([Trustpilot Smallpdf](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/smallpdf.com)) and QR codes deactivated after a trial ends ([AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/blogs/the-end-of-qr-code-subscription-traps-why-the-industry-is-shifting/)). mdothree should stay monthly by default with no card-required trial, copy Coolors' pre-renewal reminder, and promise in writing that its **static QR codes never expire**. Its privacy claim is now technically true after the Firestore fix, and it is a real edge over Smallpdf and iLovePDF, which upload files. It should be shown, not just asserted: explain how the processing works, and make the tools work offline.

## rigor.design: underpriced, over-gifted, and walled in the wrong place

Career tools cluster at **$19–$50/month list**: Jobscan $49.95, Resume Worded about $19, Rezi $29, Teal about $29, Kickresume $24, LinkedIn Premium Career from $39.99 ([Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=10428); [Rezi](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing); [Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/pricing/); [salesbread](https://salesbread.com/how-much-does-linkedin-premium-cost/)). At $9.99/mo, rigor sits below everything except Yoodli's $8 entry tier and Kickresume's annual $8/mo. Price is not the barrier. The barriers are the shape of the free tier and where sign-in sits. The dominant pattern among leaders is **"score free, fix gated."** Resume Worded shows a partial score and gates the line-by-line report ([hirequotient](https://www.hirequotient.com/blog/resume-worded-review)). Jobscan gives about 5 scans a month free and gates One-Click Optimize ([Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=10428)). Rezi allows 1 resume and 3 PDF downloads free ([Rezi](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing)).

The per-tool quota is the clearest structural mistake. Three uses on each of seven subdomains gives a determined job seeker **21 free AI generations a month**. That is more than Jobscan's or Rezi's free allowance and probably more than a typical job search needs. Pro then has little to sell, while every free generation costs real Claude API money. A **single global quota** (3–5 uses per month across the suite) aligns with the market and with costs. The sign-in wall points the other way. Evidence here is anecdotal: one founder reports signup conversion rising from about 3% to about 30% after removing a signup wall ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/clarrydaro/how-i-increased-docpilots-conversion-rate-by-removing-the-signup-wall-34l1)), and that is not a benchmark. Still, the analogs consistently get users to a score quickly. The better design is one anonymous, rate-limited run that shows the score or a partial result and retains no resume data, followed by Google one-tap sign-in to see the full fix, save it, or use the remaining quota. Server-verified Cloudflare Turnstile protects that anonymous endpoint. Its tokens are single-use, valid for 300 seconds, and give no protection unless checked through Siteverify ([Cloudflare Turnstile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/)).

Job search is episodic, and AI apps churn **30% faster** than non-AI apps while earning 41% more per payer ([SaaStr/RevenueCat 2026](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). Competitors respond with weekly plans (Teal), quarterly discounts (Jobscan, Kickresume), lifetime deals (Rezi $149, Big Interview $299) and money-back guarantees (Rezi's 30 days, Kickresume's 14 days "0 questions asked") ([Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/pricing/); [placementpreparation](https://www.placementpreparation.io/blog/best-ai-tools-for-interview-preparation/)). rigor should add a quarterly "job-search pass" (around $24.99) and a published money-back window rather than cut the monthly price. Its suite is its structural advantage, because incumbents are converging on bundles. Teal's Series A explicitly funds interview and salary-negotiation tools ([Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/teal-raises-7-5m-for-ai-careers-platform)). That advantage only works if one session and one plan cover all seven tools, with cross-tool prompts after each result ("your resume scored 72; generate a matched cover letter"). Resume privacy is a differentiator worth claiming. State at upload which LLM vendor processes the text, that API data is not used for training, and the retention period, and offer self-serve deletion, which beats Jobscan's support-ticket process ([venture-lab](https://venture-lab.org/2026/resume-scanner-data-retention-questions/amp/)). Avoid Final Round AI's "Stealth Mode" positioning for live-interview help, which invites reputational trouble ([Final Round AI](https://www.finalroundai.com/pricing)).

## Traffic comes from many intent pages on one domain, and revenue will concentrate in a few sites

Every web-first winner in these notes gets traffic from many useful entity or task pages, not from a single app page. dreamdictionary.org draws about 422K visits a month, **69.6% from Google organic** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/dreamdictionary.org/overview)). crontab.guru built a page for each cron interval ([marketingexamples](https://marketingexamples.com/seo/cronitor)). Zety's roughly **1.6M organic visits** come from example and template content ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/zety.com/competitors)). Levels.fyi gives salary data away free to sell negotiation services at $1,250–$5,000 ([Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/services?from=tech)). Two headwinds shape the target list. First, AI Overviews appeared on **13.14% of queries by late 2025**, and click-through drops to about 8% when one appears, against about 15% without ([The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-organic-traffic-crisis-analysis-report)). Pure definition queries ("Tower card meaning") therefore matter less than interactive-intent and question-shaped queries that a summary cannot replace: "yes or no tarot," "I Ching coin toss online," "merge pdf without upload," "score my resume." One AI tarot builder found people search for answers to anxieties, not definitions, and built a Love Tarot suite around "what is he thinking?" ([Indie Hackers: TarotYet](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/tarotyet)). Second, MDO3D's subdomain architecture splits authority. Google's John Mueller calls subdomains and subdirectories "essentially equivalent," but he recommends keeping content together and using subdomains only for things that are "slightly different" ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-treats-subdomains-subdirectories-john-mueller-says)). Practitioners report that consolidated domains perform better ([hawksem](https://hawksem.com/blog/subdomain-vs-subdirectory/)). At minimum, each portfolio needs a hub on its apex domain that cross-links every tool. The longer-term choice is whether to 301 the subdomains into subfolders.

The indie portfolio evidence says to expect a power law. Pieter Levels describes a failure rate of about 95% across dozens of ventures, with revenue concentrated in a handful such as Photo AI ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579)). Only **17.3% of new subscription apps reach $1K MRR within two years** ([SaaStr/RevenueCat 2026](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). For a solo operator with 28 sites, the strategy follows directly: fix payment integrity and legal basics everywhere, because they are shared templates. Instrument a visit-to-preview-to-checkout-to-paid funnel on each site. Then put SEO and pricing work into the two to four sites that show traction, and leave the rest on maintenance.

Several important unknowns remain. No published conversion benchmark exists for web, no-account, one-time reading purchases, or for blurred-preview paywalls on the web. The RevenueCat figures come from mobile installs and are directional only. Several competitor prices (Teal, Jobscan, Smallpdf, TinyWow) come from third-party aggregators that sometimes conflict. The case for per-reading pricing above $2.99 is an inference, not a tested result.

## Conclusion

The analogs change how to read MDO3D's problem. The portfolio does not need cleverer pricing. It needs to stop leaking the trust and the transactions it already earns. Each of the three business models is validated by a named, profitable analog: Co-Star's packs and Astrodienst's reports for one-time readings, Coolors for a $3–5 utility plan, and Rezi and Kickresume for bootstrapped career tools. Each also has a well-known failure (Nebula, Smallpdf's trial-to-annual charges and the QR deactivation traps, Zety) whose complaints MDO3D can turn into a selling point by being the honest option: one payment, nothing to cancel, files that never leave the browser, a resume deleted on request.

The less obvious implication concerns the solo-operator constraint. Because the highest-priority fixes (verified fulfillment, legal pages, removing invented proof, disclaimers, Turnstile) are identical across all 28 sites, they belong in one shared payment-and-trust module rather than 28 separate repairs. Once that module exists, the remaining work narrows to a data question that only MDO3D's own funnel metrics can answer: which few sites deserve the SEO and pricing effort that the power law will reward.
